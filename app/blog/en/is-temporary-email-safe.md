## Is Temporary Email Safe? What It Protects and What It Doesn't

A temporary address is safe for what it is made for: sign-ups you do not care about keeping. It is the wrong tool for anything you need to hold on to. Whether it is "safe" depends on what you are trying to protect, so it is worth being exact about both sides.

### What a temporary address protects

- **Your real inbox from marketing.** Newsletters, "we miss you" emails, and partner offers go to an address you never have to open again.
- **Your real address from data breaches.** When a site's user database leaks, the address in it is the throwaway one. It is not the address on your bank login, your work account, or anything else.
- **Your accounts from being linked together.** Companies and data brokers commonly use the email address to match records about the same person. A different address on each site breaks that match.
- **Your main address from future phishing.** The fewer places that have your main address, the fewer phishing emails are aimed at it.

### What it does not protect

- **It is not anonymity.** The website you sign up to still sees your IP address, your browser, and everything you type into it. A temporary address hides one identifier, not you.
- **It does not secure the account.** Whoever can read the inbox can usually reset the password. A temporary inbox is a weak foundation for that: it has no password of its own, and it may not be there when you need it.
- **It does not make email private.** Ordinary email is not end-to-end encrypted. The message exists on the sender's systems and on the service that receives it for you, in a form that service can read.
- **It does not make links safe.** A phishing link is just as dangerous when you open it from a temporary inbox.

### How this works on smail.pw

Temporary email services make different choices. These are ours:

- **Who can open the inbox.** An inbox opens only in the browser where the address was created, which a cookie remembers. There is no page where someone can type in an address and read its mail. Addresses are generated at random; you cannot pick one.
- **No password.** Anyone who can use that browser can read the inbox. On a shared or public computer, press **Delete address** when you are finished, so the next person cannot open it.
- **Storage.** Emails are stored on our servers so that you can read them. They are kept long-term by default, with no guarantee of how long, and they may be removed at any time.
- **How emails are displayed.** A message is shown in a sandbox: scripts inside it cannot run and forms cannot be submitted. Links open in a new tab. Images in a message are loaded straight from the sender's server, so the sender can tell that the message was opened and can see your IP address.
- **Attachments.** Only the message body is shown. Attachments are not displayed and cannot be downloaded.
- **Receive-only.** Nobody can send or reply from a smail.pw address, including you. See [can temporary email send?](/can-temporary-email-send)
- **No ads and no third-party trackers.** We set only two cookies, both needed to run the site. The details are in the [Privacy Policy](/privacy).

### Where it is a good fit

- Trying a product before you decide to give it a real address
- One-time downloads and content behind an email form
- Coupons and promo codes
- A forum or community you will read once
- Testing your own sign-up flow

### Where not to use it

- Banking, payments, and anything else that involves money
- Work and school accounts
- Government, tax, legal, and medical services
- Any account that is used to recover other accounts
- Any account you would be upset to lose
- Anything where the message itself is confidential

### A one-question test

Ask yourself: "If I could never open this inbox again, would it matter?"

If the answer is no, a temporary address is a safe choice. If the answer is yes, use an email alias or your real address. [Temporary email best practices](/blog/temporary-email-best-practices) covers the habits that keep a throwaway account from turning into one you depend on.

### Final takeaway

Temporary email protects your address, not your identity and not your account. Used for throwaway sign-ups, it is one of the simplest privacy tools there is. Used for anything you need to keep, it is a risk.
